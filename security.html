Security

Last updated 26 November 2026 · Beta — pending counsel review

Where we are. Arrowlogy is a small private beta, so this page describes the practices we have implemented honestly — no certifications claimed, no audit badges we have not earned. Where something is still being built, it says so.

1. Data in transit

This website and the beta editor are served over HTTPS. Uploads and downloads move over encrypted connections, and our hosting provider provides transport encryption and DDoS protection at the edge. If you ever reach an Arrowlogy page that is not encrypted, stop and tell us.

2. Storage and access

  • Uploaded media sits in object storage that is not publicly listable, addressed by long, unguessable keys.
  • Access to production systems is limited to the people who need it, using individual accounts rather than shared logins.
  • Secrets and keys are kept out of the website code and out of the public repository.
  • Where our storage provider supports it, media is encrypted at rest. The final storage vendor and its encryption details are still being confirmed — see Subprocessors.

3. Short retention is a security control

The cheapest way to protect data is not to keep it. Source media and intermediate files are deleted within 24 hours of a job finishing, and rendered exports are removed once you have had a reasonable chance to download them. Fewer files, kept briefly, means a smaller target. The full schedule is in our Privacy Policy.

4. Cloud workers

Transcoding, captioning and rendering run on automated workers, each handling a single job rather than holding a shared pool of everyone’s footage. Workers reach only the files for the job they are running. We do not pass your footage to a human editor, and we do not use your content to train models.

5. Accounts

  • Passwords are stored hashed, never in plain text.
  • You can sign in with Google instead of setting a password; we never see your Google credentials.
  • Use a unique password and a password manager. Tell us immediately if you suspect someone else has access.
  • Two-factor authentication is not available in the beta yet. It is on the list before wider release.

6. Providers

We use a deliberately short list of infrastructure providers: Google Workspace for email and Cloudflare for hosting and delivery, both confirmed. Object storage and speech-to-text vendors are still being finalised and will be published on the Subprocessors page before the beta widens. Each provider processes data only on our instructions.

7. If something goes wrong

If we become aware of a breach affecting your personal data, we will investigate, contain it, and notify affected users and the relevant authority as required by law. We will tell you what happened, what data was involved, and what we are doing about it — plainly, without burying it.

8. Reporting a vulnerability

If you think you have found a security issue, email hello@arrowlogy.com with the subject “Security report”. Include enough detail to reproduce it.

  • Please do not access other people’s data, run disruptive tests, or test against real user accounts.
  • Give us a reasonable window to fix the issue before disclosing it publicly.
  • We will acknowledge your report, keep you updated, and credit you if you want to be credited.
  • We do not currently run a paid bounty programme.

9. What we do not claim

We are not SOC 2 certified, not ISO 27001 certified, and not HIPAA compliant. We do not sign business-associate agreements, and the Service must not be used for medical, health, patient or government-ID content — that is a hard ban in our Acceptable Use Policy. If your work needs a regulated environment, Arrowlogy is the wrong tool for it.
